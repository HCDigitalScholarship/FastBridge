Formulas Explanation

Here you will find the explanations for the formulas used in our metrics.

DEFINITIONS

Word or Token:Every different form of the word; i.e. a “token” or N. For the purposes of these analyses, proper names (e.g., Marcus, Roma) are excluded from the calculations.
Unique Word or Type:Total words irrespective of different forms; i.e. “types” or V; e.g. bellum and belli would be two “words” or two “tokens” but only one “unique word” or “type”.
Word Length:The number of characters in a word; e.g. egerunt = 7. Six characters is the typical threshold in readability studies for a “long word.”
Hapax (legomenon):LogTTR divides the log of the number of types (V) by the log of the number of tokens (N).
Bin Frequency (# on graph):The percentage of words in text selection that fall into certain frequency bins within the Diederich 1500.

PLOTS

Word Frequency:Plots a histogram of the most common words found in text selection, no exclusions.
Cumulative Lexical Load:Assigns a lexical score to each word of the text, displays the ever-growing cumulative difficulty/load of the text.
Linear Lexical Load:Assigns a lexical score to each word, displays the change in lexical load of the text selection.
Bin Frequency:Displays the percentage of words in text selection that fall into certain frequency bins within the Diederich 1500.

READABILITY FORMULAE

LexR: LexR measures a text's lexical readability on a 10-point scale for historical languages that blends data about word length, lexical high frequency, lexical sophistication, and lexical variation.
LexR = ((mean word length * 0.217)
            + (% outside Diederich 300 * 0.058)
            + (% outside DCC * 0.067)
            + (% outside Diederich 1500 * 0.077)
            + (Lexical Density * 0.059)
            + (LogTTR * 4.993)
            + (Root TTR * 0.134)
            - 12.666) x 0.833
Where, - 12.666 is the constant to set base score at 0; the coefficient 0.833 converts the score to a 10-point scale.
PLexR:PLexR calculates a text's readability by replacing LexR's generic parameters for lexical high frequency and lexical sophistication with customized core and non-core vocabularies. [in development]
ARI:A measurement comprising word length and sentence length. A measurement comprising word length and sentence length. The Automated Readability Index (ARI) scores map onto US Grade Level.

ARI = 4.71 * (Total # of Characters / Tokens)) + (0.5 * (Tokens / Total # of Sentences) - 21.43
Coleman-Liau Index:A measurement comprising word length and sentence length. Coleman-Liau Index scores map onto US Grade Level.

CLI = (0.0588 * (Total # of Characters / Tokens) * 100) - (0.296 * ((Total # of Sentences / Tokens) * 100)) - 15.8
Dale-Chall:A measurement comprising unfamiliar vocabulary and sentence length. A score of 9 would typically indicate a college-level text; a score of 10+ would indicate post-graduate level difficulty.

DC = (0.1579 * Percentage of Difficult Words [Not in DCC Latin Core]) + (0.0496 * Average Sentence Length [+ 3.6365, if Percentage of Difficulty Word >= 5%]
Dale-Chall (New):A measurement comprising unfamiliar vocabulary and sentence length.

NDC = 64-(0.95 (Unfamiliar Tokens/Tokens * 100))-(0.69* (Tokens / total # of sentences))
LIX:A measurement comprising long words and sentence length. Texts with scores greater than 40 are considered difficult.

LIX = (Tokens / Total # of Sentences') + (Long Words * 100 / Tokens')
RIX:A simpler metric than LIX. Texts with scores greater than 2 are considered difficult.

RIX = (Long words / Total Sentences)
SMOG:A measurement comprising long words and sentence length. SMOG scores map onto US Grade Level.

SMOG = 1.043 * sqrt(Total # of Long Words * 30 / Total # of Sentences) + 3.1291
Spache:A measurement comprising sentence length and unfamiliar words, designed for texts aimed at young readers (e.g., grades 1-3 in English). A Score of 6 would be typical of texts appropriate for US 9th Grade.

Spache = (0.121 * Average Sentence Length in Words) + (0.082 * Unfamiliar Types [words outside the DCC Latin Core] / Types')) + 0.659
Lexical Density:The number of lexical or content words (nouns, adjectives, verbs, and adverbs) divided by the total number of words in a text.

LexDen = (Total Number of Nouns + Adjectives + Verbs + Adverbs + Numbers + Idioms in Text)/Tokens * 100
Lexical Sophistication:The number of unusual or advanced words in the text, defined as those words that are not in the Diederich 1500, or around the 80% vocabulary in a typical text.

LexSoph = (100 - Types in Diederich 1500)/Tokens * 100
Lexical Variation:How often individual words are repeated within a text.
TTR:The ratio of the number of unique words (“types” or V) to the total number of words (“tokens” or N). This formula exhibits bias against shorter texts.

TTR = Types / Tokens
CTTR or Corrected TTR:Corrected TTR divides the number of types (V) by the square-root of 2 times the number of tokens (N).

CTTR = Types / SQRT (Tokens * 2)
Root TTR:Root TTR divides the number of types (V) by the square-root of the number tokens.
RTTR = Types / SQRT (Tokens)
LogTTR or Herdan's C:LogTTR divides the log of the number of types (V) by the log of the number of tokens (N).

LogTTR = Types / Log (Tokens)
